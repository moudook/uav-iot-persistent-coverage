I–II. Motivation & a 3GPP-Anchored, Cross-Layer Taxonomy
I. Motivation & Scope
•  UAVs (drones): surging demand in civilian & military domains — low cost, easy deployment, minimal risk.
“A reliable wireless communication link between swarms of drones and ground base stations (BS) is essential to automate the aerial network, e.g., Flying Ad-hoc Networks (FANETs).”
•  Early MANET policies failed for aerial networking: rapidly changing topology, fast mobility, and three-dimensional free space movements.
“UAVs are characterized by greater degrees of mobility and freedom in the 3D space… in comparison to ground networks.”
“This survey provides a holistic view about the current state-of-the-art on UAV communications, identifying challenges and outlining research topics.”
•  Future → 6G + SAGSIN (Space-Air-Ground-Sea Integrated Network); enablers: AI/ML, edge, blockchain, RIS, Digital Twins, ISAC.
II. Contributions (what's new)
“It is the first to systematically integrate both academic research and industry perspectives, with a strong emphasis on 3GPP standardization (Releases 15 to 18).”
“The paper introduces a unified taxonomy that incorporates emerging paradigms such as Digital Twins (DT), Reconfigurable Intelligent Surfaces (RIS), and Integrated Sensing and Communications (ISAC).”
•  Holistic cross-layer analysis: physical → data link → network → transport layers (prior surveys focused on isolated layers).
•  Contextualizes UAV networking within future 6G and SAGSIN roadmaps.
•  Organization: I Intro → II Contributions → III Background → IV Architecture → V Design Aspects → VI Standardization → VII Testbeds → VIII Open Issues → IX Conclusion.

III
III. Background of Aerial Networking
UAS & FANET
•  UAS = 5 components: (1) UAV (air vehicle), (2) Ground Control Station, (3) Payload/data processing, (4) Control & data links, (5) Support system.
•  FANET = infrastructure-less network of flying UAV nodes.
•  One node acts as backbone UAV (connected to GCS/satellite).
•  Building blocks: topology, mobility model, node density, radio propagation, power, localization.
•  Key challenge: high-speed 3D mobility vs MANETs/VANETs.
•  Hierarchical / cluster-based FANETs for scalability & survivability.
Cooperation Schemes for Multi-UAV
•  Physical coupling: UAVs physically connected; motion of one depends on others.
•  Formations: UAVs fly a pattern (star/rectangle), maintain distance/speed/acceleration.
•  Swarms: homogeneous teams, decentralized communication → robust global behaviour.
•  Intentional cooperation: tasks allocated, predefined trajectories (centralized/decentralized).
•  Modern missions = hybrid / regime-switching systems (switch modes per mission phase).
•  UAV classification: by type (fixed-wing, rotary-wing, hybrid/VTOL); by size (small/mini, medium, large).

IV
IV. Communication, Roles, AI/ML, GenAI (A–D)
A. Communication Architectures
•  1. Direct links (ISM 2.4/5.8 GHz): simplest, but limited by LoS & range; safety/interference sensitive.
•  2. Satellite-based: wide coverage, good for NLoS/remote; but high latency (~0.12 s), heavy antennas, cost.
“Cellular-connected UAVs offer several appealing advantages… a win-win case not only for UAVs, but also for cellular networks.”
•  (a) aerial BS / flying relay = UAV-Assisted Cellular; (b) aerial user = Cellular-Connected UAV.
B. Roles of UAVs in Networks
“UAVs as base stations enables continuity of communications by expanding the communication coverage and acting as protocol converters.”
•  Challenges: energy optimization, coverage, bandwidth, trajectory/path planning, WPT, security.
•  UAV as Relay Station: extends range of existing infrastructure (disaster response, on-demand coverage).
•  UAV as Sensor/Actuator: surveillance, agriculture, forestry; low bandwidth; often onboard processing; can act as data mule.
C. AI/ML in UAV Communications
•  Optimize trajectories + radio resources jointly (power, spectrum, user association) under non-convex constraints.
•  Enhance PHY/MAC: channel estimation, beam/antenna mgmt, interference handling in 3D channels.
•  Automate network functions: mobility mgmt, routing, load balancing (single & swarm).
•  Improve security: intrusion/anomaly detection, jamming identification in multi-hop aerial networks.
D. Generative AI for A2G Channel Modeling
“The development and examination of UAV communications critically depend on precise models of electromagnetic propagation.”
•  Standardized models (3GPP TR 38.901) are terrestrial-calibrated → inadequate for aerial uptilt paths.
•  Data-driven (site-specific) & generative (non-site-specific): TTS-CGAN, MTS-CGAN for spatially consistent modeling.
•  Open challenge: spatio-temporal consistency + capturing small-scale fading in generated samples.

--- Slide 7 ---
IV
IV. IoD, Multi-UAV, RIS/ISAC, MIMO/DT (E–H)
E. Internet of Drones (IoD) & Middleware
•  IoD treats low-altitude airspace as a limited resource; ZSP (Zone Service Provider) coordinates local UAVs.
•  Airspace divided into zones with airways (flight paths) & intersections; nodes for takeoff/land/recharge.
•  Middleware gives vendor-independent unified control; simulation env. for resilient protocols.
F. Multi-UAV Architectures
•  Single-layered vs multi-layered (3D). Gateway view: C1 (star), C2 (leader gateway), C3 (mesh).
•  Leader UAV selected for stability (static leader-follower or distributed leadership).
•  Scalability: star → mesh → multi-star / hierarchical mesh.
•  Tech maturity: energy optimization = mature; trajectory/path planning = intermediate; security & trust = early stage.
G. RIS-assisted & ISAC UAV Communications
•  RIS: electronically controllable EM surfaces reflect/enhance signals; mounted on UAVs boosts A2G links.
•  6 RIS integration scenarios: data collection, ubiquitous coverage, relaying, PHY security, SWIPT, cellular-connected.
•  ISAC: joint sensing + communication — sensing aids beam tracking; comms aids mapping/positioning.
H. MIMO & Digital Twin for UAVs
•  MIMO: spectral efficiency via spatial diversity/multiplexing; challenges: pilot contamination, 3D beam tracking.
•  Solutions: interference-cancellation pilot decontamination, Kalman-filter channel tracking, dual-functional MIMO.
•  Digital Twin: virtual replica (data acquisition → processing → application); aligns with 3GPP Rel-15/16/17.
•  DT benefit: real-time monitoring, predictive maintenance, path/task adaptation, operator training.

--- Slide 8 ---
IV
IV. Self-Org, Spectrum, Channels/Protocols, Security, Optimization (I–M)
I. Self-Organization
•  Ad-hoc network formation in the sky; akin to natural flocking (Reynolds' boids).
•  Recipe: addressing → neighbor discovery → formation → routing.
J. Spectrum Management
“A major issue is the lack of a dedicated spectrum for commercial drones… especially for C2.”
•  ISM (900 MHz, 2.4, 5.8 GHz); FCC considering 5030–5091 & 60–64 GHz.
•  Sharing: Opportunistic / Competitive / Cooperative.
K. Channel & Protocols
•  Channel = large-scale (LoS/NLoS) + small-scale (multipath, Doppler).
•  GPU ray-tracing (Sionna RT) for site-specific calibration.
•  OSI: PHY (directional), MAC (full-duplex, MPR), Network (AODV/DSR), Transport (PRRT/LRT).
L. Security & C2 Requirements
•  C2 modes (TS 22.125): waypoints, stick, UTM-auto, autonomous.
•  7 requirements: Confidentiality, Integrity, Non-repudiation, Authentication, Access Control, Availability, Privacy.
M. Resource Optimization
•  3D placement: altitude ↑ LoS but ↑ path loss; solved via PSO, Genetic Algorithm, CVX, convex hull.
•  NOMA, Decode-and-Forward, water-filling, SCA, alternating minimization; graph-based Pareto/dual-ascent.

Why testbeds matter
•  Turn theory → deployable aerial networks; validate AI/ML, RIS, ISAC at scale.

IX. Conclusion
What the survey establishes
•  UAVs enable cooperative, self-organizing missions in unreachable areas.
•  Coverage: communication architecture, protocols, requirements, security; network formation & state-of-the-art solutions.
•  3GPP standardization (Rel-15–18) bridges research & commercial deployment; network slicing for UAV.
•  FANET addresses scalability of direct-link networking; communication remains the key constraint.
Where it is heading
•  Open issues + future directions push researchers toward 6G / SAGSIN-ready UAV networks.
•  Convergence of AI/ML, RIS, ISAC, Digital Twins reshapes UAV networking.
•  Standardization + real testbeds (AERPAW, SkyRAN) turn theory into deployable aerial networks.
“UAV communications has evolved from a niche topic to a key enabler of future wireless connectivity.”

Course Problem Statement (Week 1)
Given problem (verbatim)
•  Develop an AI-based system for deploying multiple UAVs as temporary aerial base stations.
•  The system should determine where UAVs should be positioned and how they should move as users move,
•  while maintaining UAV-to-UAV connectivity, maximizing coverage, and minimizing energy consumption.
•  Input: User locations/density, UAV positions, battery levels, communication range, channel quality.
•  Output: UAV positions/movement decisions and resulting coverage/connectivity.
•  AI/ML: PSO / Genetic Algorithm for placement + Multi-Agent Q-Learning for dynamic formation.
