## Part 1: Praise — What This Paper Does Exceptionally Well

1. **Uncompromising Scientific Honesty (Turning a Negative Result into a Contribution):**
Most student (and many published) MARL papers tweak hyperparameters, cherry-pick random seeds, or cripple baselines until their neural network wins. Instead, when your parameter-shared CTDE MAPPO controller ($CK = 0.662$) lost to a hand-designed unprivileged reactive heuristic ($CK = 0.972$) by $0.309$, you turned the report into a **rigorous diagnostic investigation**. Using paired, seed-matched controlled arms to bound how much of the gap could be explained by observation design, energy constraints, training budget, or local vs. centralized critics—and showing that every single confidence interval falls short of the $0.309$ gap—is first-rate empirical science.


2. **The Feasibility-Certified Evaluation Framework (Section III-G):**
Proving via the packing lemma ($N \ge K \cdot P(A, R_{cov})$) that the original $N=5, K=2, R_{cov}=35\text{ m}$ setup passes the conventional area-surplus check ($\sigma = 1.503 \ge 1$) yet is *provably geometrically infeasible* for covering the region ($N \ge 4K = 8$) is a sharp, clean theoretical contribution. Pairing this with a teleporting placement oracle and exposing the degeneracy of the binary Service Continuity metric ($SC(\pi) \equiv 0$ when $\mathcal{F} = \emptyset$) gives the paper a clear methodological hook.


3. **Diagnostic & Negative-Control Discipline:**
* Catching that INET’s `minSnir` statistic responded to transmit power ($P_{tx}$) across a $117\text{ dB}$ sweep but remained invariant to receiver distance ($10\text{ m}$ to $80\text{ m}$)—and therefore **refusing to claim runtime cross-validation**, limiting the claim to algebraic path-loss equivalence ($0.012\text{ dB}$)—shows rare experimental integrity.


* Discovering via the "Distinct actions" and "Stranded UAV" diagnostics that (a) omitting agent IDs caused permutation-symmetry collapse and (b) the safety filter's original energy rule allowed departures on a flat battery (worth $+0.078$ coverage once repaired) demonstrates real systems debugging rather than black-box curve fitting.


* The **automated claim audit** (`claim_audit.py`, 38 items) and **plasticity probe** (testing forward-dormant vs. gradient-silent neurons in Table XX) go well beyond standard course expectations.




4. **Up-to-Date, Comparative Literature Survey:**
Splitting the survey into core foundations (Table I) and explicit "Near-Misses" (Table II, including very recent 2025–2026 works) makes your novelty boundary transparent and defensible.

### 4. dBW vs. dBm Unit Confusion in Table XIX (Page 28)

* **The Issue:** In Table XIX, you list the transmit powers as:
* Config A: `0.5 W (-3 dBm)`

* Config B: `1 mW (-30 dBm)`

* Config C: `1 uW (-60 dBm)`

* Config D: `1 pW (-120 dBm)`


Those parenthetical numbers are **dBW** (decibels relative to $1\text{ W}$), not **dBm** (decibels relative to $1\text{ mW}$)! Specifically, $0.5\text{ W} = +27\text{ dBm}$ ($-3\text{ dBW}$), $1\text{ mW} = 0\text{ dBm}$ ($-30\text{ dBW}$), $1\ \mu\text{W} = -30\text{ dBm}$ ($-60\text{ dBW}$), and $1\text{ pW} = -90\text{ dBm}$ ($-120\text{ dBW}$).


* **The Fix:** Change `dBm` to `dBW` in Table XIX, or update the numbers to `+27 dBm`, `0 dBm`, `-30 dBm`, and `-90 dBm`.
