I reviewed the uploaded report in detail and cross-checked the core novelty claims against related UAV/MARL/coverage literature. My honest assessment is:

This is not “just a standard MAPPO UAV project.” The strongest part is genuinely research-like: your feasibility-certified evaluation framework.
But the MAPPO controller itself is not yet a strong algorithmic contribution, because your own experiments show a hand-designed reactive rule beats it badly. The work is most defensible as a methodological / evaluation-framework paper with a negative MARL result, not as “we propose a superior learned UAV controller.”

Below is a deep technical review.

1. High-level verdict
What you did well
Your report has several unusually strong aspects for an R&D/student project:

You discovered that the original configuration was infeasible.
The observation that the conventional area-surplus criterion passes while the corner-packing bound rejects the configuration is important. This is one of the best parts of the work.

You separated “controller failure” from “task infeasibility.”
Many UAV/RL papers report coverage ratios without proving that full coverage was ever achievable. Your oracle/certificate idea directly addresses that weakness.

You reported a negative result honestly.
You did not hide that the reactive rule beats MAPPO. That is scientifically stronger than forcing a weak learning result to look good.

You added serious audit/reproducibility machinery.
Automated claim audits, deterministic seed handling, path-loss checks, and negative controls are far above typical course-project quality.

You correctly identify credit assignment as a likely bottleneck.
The result that a shared team reward may fail in persistent multi-UAV coverage is plausible and connects to known MARL credit-assignment issues. COMA, for example, was explicitly motivated by multi-agent credit assignment and uses a counterfactual baseline to isolate an agent’s contribution Source.

What is weak or risky
The novelty is overclaimed in places.
The packing bound itself is not a new mathematical invention; it is a standard covering/packing lower-bound idea. Your innovation is its application as an evaluation certificate for UAV-IoT persistent K-coverage, not the lemma itself.

The MAPPO result is too undertrained to support broad claims.
25 episodes / 8 seeds is not enough to conclude “MAPPO is bad for this problem.” You can conclude: under this implementation, training budget, reward, observation, and architecture, MAPPO underperformed a reactive rule.

The “decentralized execution” claim is weakened by the global safety/action mask.
If the connectivity mask uses global true state at execution time, then the actor is decentralized but the system is not fully decentralized. You should call it:
“decentralized actor with centralized safety-filter / supervisor.”

The reactive heuristic may not be a fair opponent unless carefully described.
If it uses exact coverage deficits, exact sensor states, or global geometry, then it may be more privileged than the learned policy. Your “local assignment” version helps, but reviewers will still ask whether the heuristic gets cleaner information than MAPPO.

The physical model is over-detailed relative to how much it affects the result.
Your own audit says the link margin is huge, so the PHY is not the binding constraint. That is fine, but then avoid implying that PHY realism is central to the result.

2. Is this innovation or already discovered?
Short answer
Partly innovation, partly recombination, partly rediscovery.

The pieces individually already exist:

UAV coverage with energy-aware DRL already exists.
Persistent UAV coverage with recharging already exists.
Action masking in RL already exists and is formally studied; invalid action masking is known to be a valid technique in policy-gradient algorithms Source.
MAPPO is a known cooperative MARL baseline and has been shown to work well on standard multi-agent benchmarks Source.
Joint UAV coverage, connectivity, and charging existed before your work; Trotta et al. propose centralized and distributed strategies for stationary UAV coverage, connectivity, and energy replenishment Source.
K-coverage and recharging in UAV-IoT networks is also studied; Song et al. explicitly address assigning UAVs for K-coverage and recharging in IoT networks Source.
Persistent multi-UAV coverage with priority, outage memory, finite battery, recharging, and MARL is close to your topic; a recent POGS-QMIX paper studies persistent geometric multi-UAV service coverage with finite battery and recharging, using priority/outage guidance and return-energy masks Source.
Where your work is more original
Your strongest originality is not “we use MAPPO for UAVs.” That is not enough.

Your strongest originality is:

A feasibility-certified evaluation protocol for persistent redundant UAV coverage, combining a packing-number lower bound, a teleporting oracle ceiling, and non-degenerate metrics to avoid misreading infeasible experiments.

That is a legitimate research angle.

However, phrase it carefully:

Do not say: “We invented packing-based feasibility.”
Do say: “We integrate a packing-based infeasibility certificate into the evaluation protocol for persistent K-coverage UAV-IoT experiments, and show that a commonly used area-surplus criterion can be misleading.”
That is much more defensible.

4. Main technical mistakes / weaknesses
4.1 You should not present MAPPO as the successful proposed solution
Right now the report sometimes sounds like:

“We propose a MAPPO controller for persistent coverage.”

But your results say:

“MAPPO loses badly to the reactive rule.”

So the paper’s framing should change.

Better framing:

“We propose a feasibility-certified evaluation framework and use it to audit a CTDE MAPPO controller. The audit reveals that, on the corrected feasible configuration, a hand-designed reactive rule substantially outperforms MAPPO.”

This is much stronger and more honest.

4.2 Training budget is too small for a strong MARL conclusion
A reviewer will attack this immediately.

You report roughly 8 training seeds and 25 episodes. For MAPPO, that is very small. MAPPO papers usually train over large numbers of environment steps and perform careful hyperparameter tuning. The original MAPPO work reports strong results across standard cooperative benchmarks, but that strength depends on careful implementation and sufficient training Source.

So your conclusion should be limited:

Bad claim:

“MAPPO cannot solve persistent UAV-IoT coverage.”

Better claim:

“Under the tested observation, reward, masking, and training budget, our MAPPO implementation does not match a reactive rule.”

Even better:

“This motivates future work on per-agent credit assignment, stronger architectures, curriculum learning, and centralized/MPC baselines.”

4.3 The reactive rule may be unfairly strong
The reactive rule seems to know where under-covered sensors are and moves toward them. That is exactly the high-level structure the learned policy struggles to discover.

If the heuristic uses global sensor coverage state, while MAPPO receives a compressed deficit grid, then the comparison is not purely “learning vs rule.” It is partly:

exact symbolic state access,
handcrafted assignment,
privileged geometry,
deterministic reactive behavior,
versus a learned decentralized policy with partial observation.

Your local-assignment reactive variant helps a lot, especially because it still performs extremely well. But you need to be very explicit:

What information does the reactive rule receive?
Is it the same information as MAPPO?
Does it use true per-sensor n 
d
​

Does it know all sensor positions?
Does it know global UAV positions?
Does it use centralized assignment?
If yes, call it a strong oracle-like heuristic baseline, not a fair decentralized peer.

4.4 “Decentralized execution” is overstated
Your actor may be decentralized, but the safety filter/mask appears to use the simulator’s true global state to reject actions that would disconnect the swarm.

That means execution is not purely decentralized.

Better terminology:

“CTDE actor with centralized safety-filtered execution.”

or

“Decentralized policy executed under a centralized feasibility shield.”

This matters. A reviewer could say: if the mask needs global connectivity, then your deployed system still requires a central supervisor.

4.6 The physical-layer model is mostly non-binding
You include Al-Hourani LoS probability, path loss, 802.11 MCS gating, OMNeT++/INET audit, etc. The Al-Hourani model is a known air-to-ground model for optimizing LAP/UAV altitude for coverage Source. 3GPP TR 36.777 is also a standard reference for enhanced LTE support for aerial vehicles Source.

But your own audit shows huge link margins, so the radio model does not materially affect the central result. That is not bad, but the narrative should say:

“At the chosen parameters, the radio gate is present but non-binding; geometry and fleet feasibility dominate.”

Otherwise it feels like physical-layer realism is being used rhetorically more than scientifically.

4.7 Energy/charging assumptions are not realistic enough
You correctly admit that the charging rate is about 3.6C and is a modeling assumption. That is good.

But then you should avoid claiming realism. Say:

“The energy model is a stress-test abstraction designed to make charging contention occur within a tractable episode, not a calibrated battery model.”

Also, if the episode is 400s and hover endurance is 600s, the battery stress depends heavily on movement duty cycle and charge rate. A reviewer may ask: are you studying persistent operation, or just a short stress episode?

You can defend it, but you need to make the scope modest.

4.8 You need stronger baselines
The baselines are decent for a project, but not enough for a paper-level claim.

You should add or discuss:

Centralized MPC / greedy assignment baseline
A centralized controller that directly optimizes current deficit, connectivity, and energy.

MILP or CP-SAT placement/repositioning oracle for small cases
This would provide a stronger upper bound than a teleporting placement oracle.

QMIX / VDN / QTRAN-style baselines
Persistent multi-UAV coverage with energy and outage guidance has been studied with QMIX-style value factorization; the POGS-QMIX work is especially close Source.

COMA / counterfactual advantage baseline
Since you diagnose credit assignment, you should test at least one method designed for credit assignment. COMA explicitly addresses multi-agent credit assignment using a counterfactual baseline Source.

Graph neural network / attention policy
UAV swarms have permutation and spatial structure. A shared MLP with one-hot identity may be weaker than a graph/equivariant policy. Recent UAV swarm coverage work explicitly uses symmetry-informed MARL for communication coverage Source.

4.9 The claim audit sounds stronger than it is
You say 38 items, 0 failing. But your abstract also says the audit does not audit Section VIII results.

That can confuse readers.

Be precise:

“The audit verifies model, protocol, physical-layer algebra, feasibility, and determinism claims. It does not independently validate the statistical performance conclusions of Section VIII beyond recomputing artifact-generated numbers.”

Otherwise it sounds like the audit proves the experimental claims are scientifically correct, when it mostly proves that numbers are reproducibly generated.

5.2 Teleporting oracle is an upper bound only for coverage, not full service
You already mention this, but it should be emphasized.

A teleporting static placement oracle upper-bounds instantaneous coverage. It does not necessarily upper-bound:

recovery time,
energy-feasible persistence,
connectivity-maintaining trajectories,
charging contention,
service continuity over a trajectory.
So call it:

“coverage achievability ceiling”

not:

“overall task optimum.”

5.3 Region coverage vs sensor-set coverage must be kept separate
This is one of the most important distinctions.

Your original N=5 configuration is provably infeasible for covering the entire region. But your actual experiments evaluate 20 discrete sensors. For a discrete random sensor set, N=5 may often be feasible.

You do acknowledge this, which is good. Keep hammering it:

Region certificate: N=5 impossible.
Sensor-set certificate: depends on actual layout.
Your random-draw result: N=5 excluded for 13% of 30 layouts.
Do not conflate these.

5.4 Tightness construction needs all constraints
You say N=8 is exactly sufficient for region K-coverage. Geometrically, yes.

But your full system also has:

safety distance,
communication range,
radio gate,
no-articulation / H-hop mask,
starting cluster,
finite battery,
charging,
reserve behavior.
So distinguish:

“Geometrically tight for static region K-coverage.”

from:

“Dynamically achievable under the simulator.”

You claim the audit checks connected placement and safety-mask admissibility. Good. Make that explicit immediately after the tightness claim.

6. Literature positioning: what is already known?
Already known / not novel
MAPPO itself
MAPPO is not your contribution. It is a known algorithm. The standard reference shows PPO-style methods can be strong in cooperative multi-agent benchmarks Source.

Action masking
Action masking is not novel. It has been formally studied in policy-gradient algorithms and shown to produce valid gradients under the masking setup Source.

Time-limit truncation handling
Your distinction between termination and truncation is correct, but not novel. Pardo et al. discuss why bootstrapping should be handled carefully when episodes end because of time limits Source.

UAV coverage + charging
Trotta et al. already jointly consider coverage, connectivity, and charging strategies for distributed UAV networks, including centralized optimal and distributed scheduling/positioning approaches Source.

K-coverage + recharging
Song et al. study methods to assign UAVs for K-coverage and recharging in IoT networks Source.

Persistent energy-aware multi-UAV coverage with MARL
Recent work such as POGS-QMIX studies persistent geometric multi-UAV service coverage with finite batteries, recharging, outage memory, return-energy action masks, multi-seed evaluation, and sensitivity studies Source.

What may be novel
Your novelty is the combination of evaluation devices:

packing-based infeasibility certificate,
corrected feasible configuration,
teleporting coverage oracle,
non-degenerate metrics,
explicit demonstration that area-surplus and binary service-continuity can mislead,
audit-backed negative result for MAPPO.
That is a valid niche.

But do not claim you are the first to do coverage, charging, connectivity, failure, and MARL unless your literature search is very strong. Phrase it as:

“We are not aware of prior work combining these five elements with a feasibility-certified evaluation protocol.”

That is safer.

7. Paper-writing issues
7.1 The report is too defensive
The writing repeatedly says things like:

“we do not claim…”
“the framework earns its keep…”
“the difference between a number and a measurement”
“the cheapest test does not resolve it”
“this is not cosmetic”
This is intellectually honest, but the tone can become argumentative. Academic writing should be calmer.

Replace:

“The framework is the difference between a number and a measurement.”

with:

“The framework provides the feasibility context needed to interpret reported coverage values.”

Replace:

“The area-surplus criterion certifies nothing.”

with:

“The area-surplus criterion is not sufficient for pointwise K-coverage.”

7.2 Too many contributions
You list 11 contributions. That is too many.

Compress to 4–5:

Unified UAV-IoT persistent K-coverage model with energy, connectivity, charging, and failure recovery.
Feasibility-certified evaluation framework using packing lower bound and teleporting coverage oracle.
CTDE MAPPO controller with safety mask, charging arbitration, and reserve recovery.
Non-degenerate evaluation protocol and reproducible audit.
Negative empirical result: reactive rule outperforms MAPPO; ablations diagnose possible causes.
7.3 Avoid “we proved” for empirical things
Use:

“measured,”
“observed,”
“under the tested setting,”
“consistent with,”
“does not rule out.”
You already often do this, but keep it consistent.

10. My final judgment
Is your project valid R&D?
Yes. Definitely.

Is it publishable as currently framed?
Probably not yet, because the claims are too broad and the MAPPO result is weak.

Could it become a good paper?
Yes, if you frame it as:

“Feasibility-certified evaluation for persistent redundant UAV-IoT coverage: why naive coverage metrics mislead, and what a MAPPO negative result teaches us.”

That is much stronger than:

“A MAPPO framework for fault-tolerant UAV-IoT coverage.”

What is the actual contribution?
Your actual contribution is:

You built a rigorous evaluation and auditing framework that reveals when UAV coverage experiments are infeasible or misleading, and you used it to show that a standard MAPPO controller underperforms a reactive rule on a corrected feasible persistent K-coverage task.

That is real research value.

What is not yet proven?
You have not proven:

that MAPPO cannot solve the task,
that your learned controller is competitive,
that credit assignment is definitely the cause,
that your physical model materially changes the conclusion,
that your approach is fully decentralized,
that no prior work has combined similar elements.
Best one-sentence summary
This is a strong evaluation-methodology project with an honest negative MARL result; its main innovation is the feasibility-certified measurement framework, not the MAPPO controller.
