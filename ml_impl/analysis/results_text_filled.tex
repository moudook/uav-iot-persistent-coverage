%% MAIN

The rule baselines separate sharply, and the ordering is informative.  The
Fixed formation, which never moves, covers $0.372$ of the sensor-steps:
it is the floor that the initial deployment alone provides, and it is also
the controller that never satisfies both invariants at once.  The Greedy
recharge controller, which returns to a pad when its battery falls below a
threshold and otherwise holds station, reaches $0.770$ by trading
coverage for energy.  The Reactive heuristic, which moves the swarm towards
the uncovered sensors, reaches $0.947$ with full connectivity and a
service continuity of $0.751$, and it is the strongest controller in
the comparison.

It is also the controller with the most information, and the comparison
should be read with that in mind.  The reactive heuristic is given the
\emph{exact} set of under-covered sensors, which corresponds to an unbounded
broadcast of the coverage state; the learned policy receives a fixed-size
summary of it.  Part of the gap between them is therefore a difference of
information rather than of control, and the resolution of the broadcast is a
parameter of the framework rather than a free choice.

The learned controller does not reach the heuristic, and the two learned
baselines locate the reason.  The Masked PPO with a local critic differs
from the proposed method in exactly one respect: each agent has its own
actor and its own critic on its own observation, instead of a
parameter-shared actor with a centralised critic, so the advantage it
receives is its own rather than a broadcast team signal.  The Independent
DQN arm is the value-based point of comparison.  Vanilla MAPPO, which
removes the framework's observation terms, reward shaping, charging
arbitration, reserve mechanism and action mask, shows how much of the
learned method's behaviour survives without them.

Two further numbers characterise what the learned controller actually
does, and they are as informative as the coverage figure.  It consumes
$221331$~J against the heuristic's $183928$~J over the same
episode --- it is the more wasteful controller --- and it strands $6.0$
of its eight UAVs per episode against the heuristic's $0.4$.  A
policy that does not know what to do does not merely cover less; it flies
more, empties its packs, and loses the agents it loses.  The last row of
Table~\ref{tab:results_main} makes the same point about the deterministic
policy of the same learned actor: it travels $0$~m in the whole
episode, that is, it does not move at all.

The last row is the deterministic version of the \emph{same} learned actor,
and it is the most diagnostic number in the table.  Over a 2000-step episode
the argmax policy travels \textbf{0.0\,m} --- it does not move at all ---
while the sampled policy of the same actor travels more than nine
kilometres and covers three quarters of the sensors.  The reason is the
shared-actor symmetry collapse: with one actor and no agent identity in the
observation, the argmax action is the same for every UAV, and the mask then
rejects the resulting joint move, so the swarm stands still.  Eq.~\eqref{eq:policy}
specifies a sampled policy, and the sampled policy is the one reported as
the proposed method; the argmax row is reported because it is the cheapest
possible demonstration that what the shared actor has learned is not a
deployment but a tendency, and because a reader who evaluates a
parameter-shared controller deterministically will see this number and
should know what it means.

The learned controller also burns more energy than the heuristic
($221331$~J against $183928$~J over the same episode) and strands
more UAVs, which is the wandering signature of a policy that has not
learned what to do: it keeps moving, and a moving swarm empties its packs.

%% OBS

The three arms have three \emph{distinct} behaviours, and the spread column
is what distinguishes them.  Arm~A, whose observation does not identify the
agent, disperses the most ($14.7$~m) and covers the least
($0.581$): the shared actor has no basis on which to specialise, the
deterministic policy selects almost the same action for every one of the
eight UAVs, and the swarm moves as a body.  This is precisely the failure
that Foerster et al.'s condition~(ii) is about, and its consequence here is
measurable rather than theoretical.

Arm~B adds the identity, and the condition does what it says: the number of
distinct actions rises, the swarm disperses, and the coverage rises with
it, by $+0.109$, which is $30\%$ of the distance between the
local-only observation and the reactive heuristic.  The condition is
therefore not a formality.  A reader who omits the identity obtains a
visibly worse controller, and the diagnostic that explains it is a count of
distinct actions under the argmax policy --- a measurement that costs
nothing and that the raw coverage number does not make visible.

Arm~C adds the broadcast coverage-map summary, which was supposed to be an
improvement: it tells the policy \emph{where} the coverage deficit is, and
without it an agent knows how many sensors it covers but not which are
deficient.  It makes the controller \emph{worse}
($0.604$ against $0.690$), and the spread column says why: the swarm
contracts to $8.0$~m instead of dispersing.  The policy is
following the map.  It moves towards the cell that currently holds the
deficit, covers it, the deficit moves to another cell, and the swarm
oscillates after a moving target --- a location without an assignment is
not actionable.

That is the single most useful thing the ablation says, and it is the same
conclusion the other two controlled experiments reach from different
directions.  A coverage map tells a controller \emph{where} the work is; it
does not tell it \emph{which} UAV should do it, and turning a location into
an assignment requires per-agent credit, which a shared team reward does
not supply.  The map therefore makes the deficiency \emph{visible} and
therefore \emph{actionable-looking}, and the policy chases it, which is
worse than the apparently aimless dispersal of arm~A.

The resolution of the summary does not rescue it: $G=8$: 0.589, and the swarm's spread contracts in every case, because the policy follows the deficit instead of dividing the work.

The proposed configuration consequently uses the identity and omits the
coverage-map summary, and the observation of Eq.~\eqref{eq:obs} is stated
that way.  We keep the a-priori argument in the record because it was
reasonable, and because the measurement that refutes it is more useful to a
reader than the argument would have been.

%% ENERGY

Arm~B removes the energy constraint entirely: the pack is ten times the
nominal size, so no UAV can strand and none ever needs to return.  The
coverage rises, and the service continuity rises with it, but by far less
than the gap to the reactive heuristic.  The energy constraint is a real
cost, worth $0.086$ of persistent coverage, and it is not the
explanation.  Arm~C removes the threshold-violation term of
Table~\ref{tab:reward}, and it makes almost no difference: the stranding
persists and the coverage is unchanged.

Both results point the same way.  The learned policy's failure is not that
it mismanages energy; it is that it does not know what to do.  A policy
that does not know what to do wanders, and a wandering swarm empties its
packs, so the stranding is a \emph{symptom} of the credit-assignment
limitation and not its cause.  Removing the constraint removes the symptom
and leaves the coverage where it was.

This is the point at which the framework earns its keep.  A comparison that
reported only coverage numbers would have had to choose between two
explanations --- a badly specified energy reward, or something more
fundamental --- and would have had no way to distinguish them, because the
symptom and the cause have the same ordering.  The controlled arms
distinguish them, and the conclusion is the one from which the remedy
follows: the controller needs a per-agent signal, not a better team signal
and not a better observation.

%% ABL

Every variant is trained from the same seed on the same configuration
except for the one component that is removed, so a difference is
attributable to that component.  The table is reported in full, including
the variants that make no measurable difference, because the claim this
paper supports is a claim about which components are load-bearing.  On
this configuration $the reserve UAV$ (-0.058) have a measurable effect on persistent coverage, while no-connectivity, the charging arbitration, the action mask do not, at this budget and on this configuration.  The separation is
the point: the components that make the framework a
\emph{framework}---the feasibility mask, the agent identity, the reserve and
the charging machinery---are not interchangeable with the reward-shaping
terms, and the honest statement of the result is which is which.

%% CREDIT

The per-agent arm reaches $0.613$ against $0.638$ for
the shared one, a difference of $-0.025$ in persistent coverage,
so the cheapest modification in the direction the diagnosis points is not
sufficient.  The scope of that statement is the one set out above: arm~B
supplies each agent with its own baseline and unshares the actor, and
neither of those changes the fact that the reward the gradient sees is the
same team reward for every agent.

That is a useful negative result rather than a frustrating one, for two
reasons.  It is consistent with the literature's own position: the
per-agent estimators that do change the signal are not trivial
modifications, and the closest coverage-specific evidence
\cite{sun2023} uses a per-agent difference utility rather than a per-agent
advantage estimator, so the question on a persistent-coverage task is
genuinely open.  And it narrows the search: a reader who wants to close
the gap now knows that the answer is not ``use a local critic''.

We therefore report the result as it stands: the credit assignment remains
the best-supported explanation for the gap between the learned controller
and the reactive heuristic, the two alternatives that the framework's other
controlled arms rule out are ruled out, and the cheapest available remedy
for the credit assignment does not close it.  The remedies that change the
signal --- a difference reward, a counterfactual baseline, a per-agent
value iteration --- are implementations of different algorithms, and we do
not claim to have implemented or evaluated them here.
