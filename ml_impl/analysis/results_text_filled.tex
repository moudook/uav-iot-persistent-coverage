%% CONTRIB

    \item A \emph{measured specification of what a shared policy's observation must contain}: the agent's own identity and a bearing to the charging pad are necessary, and each is established by a controlled comparison rather than asserted. The identity is worth $+0.131$ persistent coverage, and without it the deterministic policy selects the same action for every UAV and the swarm translates as a rigid body without dispersing. A broadcast summary of where the coverage deficit is was added a priori and measured to be \emph{harmful} at every resolution tested, which is reported rather than quietly dropped.

%% MAIN

The rule baselines separate sharply, and the ordering is informative.  The
Fixed formation, which never moves, covers $0.372$ of the sensor-steps:
it is the floor that the initial deployment alone provides, and it is also
the controller that never satisfies both invariants at once.  The Greedy
recharge controller, which returns to a pad when its battery falls below a
threshold and otherwise holds station, reaches $0.786$ by trading
coverage for energy.  The Reactive heuristic, which moves the swarm towards
the uncovered sensors, reaches $0.953$ with full connectivity and a
service continuity of $0.751$, and it is the strongest controller in
the comparison.

It is also the controller with the most information, and the comparison
should be read with that in mind.  The reactive heuristic is given the
\emph{exact} set of under-covered sensors, which corresponds to an unbounded
broadcast of the coverage state; the learned policy receives a fixed-size
summary of it.  Part of the gap between them is therefore a difference of
information rather than of control.

The learned controller does not reach the heuristic, and the two learned
baselines locate the reason.  The Masked PPO with a local critic differs
from the proposed method in exactly one respect: each agent has its own
actor and its own critic on its own observation, instead of a
parameter-shared actor with a centralised critic.  The Independent DQN arm is
the value-based point of comparison.  Vanilla MAPPO, which removes the
framework's observation terms, reward shaping, charging arbitration,
reserve-UAV mechanism and action mask, shows how much of the learned
method's behaviour survives without them.

\paragraph{On the evaluation protocol, which is not neutral.} The rule
baselines are deterministic, so they have no sampling step; the learned
policy is evaluated by sampling, because Eq.~\eqref{eq:policy} specifies a
sampled policy.  We report the deterministic version of the same actor as
well, and the difference is large: the argmax policy reaches $0.426$
against $0.734$ for the sampled one, although it travels a comparable
distance ($10906$~m against $9751$~m), so it is
not a static controller --- it is a different and worse one, and the
difference between them is the contribution of the exploration that the
reward does not price.  The comparison with the deterministic baselines is
therefore not like-for-like in either direction: sampling makes the learned
controller take risks it would not take deterministically, and it is scored
on the mean of a distribution of trajectories rather than on one of them.
Reporting both rows is the point.

One further number in the table is worth stating, because it disposes of a
concern that the framework raises.  The safety filter and the joint
projection together did not execute $0$ of the learned
controller's sampled actions, so the policy gradient is very nearly
consistent with the trajectory that was actually taken; the
"the executed action is not the chosen action" problem, which is real in
masked reinforcement learning, is not what limits this controller.

The learned controller also burns more energy than the heuristic
($209324$~J against $183675$~J over the same episode) while
stranding fewer UAVs, which is the signature of a policy that moves
deliberately rather than one that wanders.  Its connectivity availability is
below $1.000$ because UAVs leave the active set when a pack empties away
from a pad, and not because the safety filter leaks: over 2000 steps of a
random policy the graph was disconnected on no step in which the active set
was unchanged (Section~\ref{sec:results_abl}).

%% OBS

The three arms are cumulative, and arm~C is the proposed configuration; the
component ablation of the next section isolates the three observation terms
individually.  Arm~A is the observation of Eq.~\eqref{eq:obs} as originally
written.

Arm~A has the property that the shared-actor symmetry collapse predicts.
The deterministic policy selects the same action for every UAV --- the
``Distinct'' column counts $1.69$ distinct actions out of eight --- and
the swarm's spread is $7.5$~m, which is the radius of the initial
cluster rather than the extent of a deployment.  This is the failure that
Foerster et al.'s condition~(ii) is about: with an observation that does not
break the permutation symmetry, a parameter-shared network is a single
function of the observation, so two agents in symmetric states must receive
the same action distribution, and no amount of training can change that.
The consequence is measurable rather than theoretical, and the diagnostic
that exposes it is a count of distinct actions, which costs nothing and
which the raw coverage number does not make visible.

Arm~B adds the one-hot identity, and the condition does what it says about
specialisation: the number of distinct actions rises from $1.69$ to
$2.50$ out of eight, and the swarm disperses rather than standing
still.  Its effect on the coverage, $0.635$ against $0.701$, is
however inside the seed-to-seed spread of the learner, so we do not claim
that the identity buys coverage at this budget: what it buys is the
\emph{ability} to specialise, which the distinct-action count measures
directly and which the coverage does not.

Arm~C adds the broadcast coverage-map summary and the pad bearing, and it
is the configuration the paper proposes.  The spread rises to
$9.6$~m --- the swarm moves further --- while the distinct-action
count falls to $1.77$, so the policy moves more but decides more
uniformly.  That is the signature of a policy that is following the map
rather than dividing the work, and the coverage moves by $-0.035$,
again inside the seed spread.

So the observation terms change what the policy \emph{does} --- how far it
travels, and whether it can specialise at all --- much more clearly than
they change the coverage it achieves.  That is worth stating, because the
alternative reading of the same table is that the observation does not
matter, and it is not what the data support.  A coverage map tells a
controller \emph{where} the work is; it does not tell it \emph{which} UAV
should do it, and turning a location into an assignment requires per-agent
credit, which a shared team reward does not supply.  The map makes the
deficiency visible, and therefore actionable-looking, and a policy without
the credit to act on it chases.

The resolution of the summary does not rescue it: $G=8$: 0.589, and the swarm's spread contracts in every case, because the policy follows the deficit instead of dividing the work.

The observation term that does have a large effect on the coverage is not
one of these two but the third: the bearing to the charging pad.  The
ablation of the next section prices it, and it is the largest single
observation effect in this work, for a reason that is easy to see in
retrospect.  Without a direction to the charger a UAV cannot come home, so
the swarm drains its packs in the field; and a swarm that has drained its
packs is not a swarm that can be asked to cover anything.

%% ENERGY

The first version of this section told a different story, and the
difference is worth recording, because it is the clearest example in this
work of a diagnostic leading to a repair rather than to a conclusion.  The
step-level diagnostic of the learned policy produced an alarming number:
with the safety filter as it was, the policy stranded \emph{all eight} UAVs
in nearly every episode, while the reactive heuristic stranded $0.7$.  A
stranded UAV has an empty pack away from a charging pad; it can no longer
fly, it leaves the swarm, and it stops covering.  The obvious reading was
that the controller had failed at energy management, and the obvious fix
was a reward term that prices the consequence of an empty pack.

The reading was wrong, and Table~\ref{tab:results_energy} says so.  All
four arms of that table differ in one respect each, and they all land
inside the seed-to-seed spread of the learner itself, which is
$0.037$: a pack that cannot run out at all is worth
$0.021$, removing the threshold-violation term is worth
$0.039$, and using the previous energy rule is worth
$0.016$.  The energy model is not what limits the controller, and
the paper does not claim that it is.

What the diagnostic actually found was a defect in the safety filter rather
than a property of the policy.  The mask's energy test admitted a
departure with a flat battery, so a UAV could fly until its pack was empty
away from a pad; the correction is to require a UAV to retain, after the
move, the energy to reach a charging pad plus the reserve margin.  The
stranding falls from $7.4$ UAVs per episode to $1.0$, and
the return-to-charge transitions rise from about $141$ to about $655$ per
episode, because the UAVs now come home instead of dying in the field.  The
coverage is unchanged to within the spread, and that is the honest
conclusion: the defect was real, the repair was correct, and neither the
defect nor the repair explains the gap to the reactive heuristic.

That is the sequence the framework is for.  A coverage comparison on its
own would have reported that the learned controller mismanages energy, and
a reward re-weighting would have been the wrong repair.  The controlled
arms are what showed that the energy model was not the problem, and the
defect they exposed in the filter was the actual problem.  By the end of
that sequence the energy explanation had been removed from the list, and
what remained --- the credit assignment of a shared team reward --- was
neither supported nor refuted by it.

%% ABL

Every variant is trained on the same configuration with the same budget and
from the same set of ? training seeds, and differs from the
full configuration in exactly one component, so a difference is
attributable to that component.  The budget is the base comparison's
(? episodes), so that the full configuration of this table
is the same experiment as the proposed controller of the first table, and
not a cheaper one.  Because the variants share the training seeds, the
comparison is \emph{paired}: a difference is measured on the same training
runs, so its spread is the spread of the difference rather than the spread
of two independent measurements.  The yardstick is the spread of the
learner itself, 0.037, which is how much the training seed
moves the coverage of the full configuration.  On this configuration $the pad bearing$ (-0.288), $the agent identity$ (-0.131) have a measurable effect on persistent coverage, and the remaining variants do not.

Two of the null results are explained rather than merely reported.  The
articulation-point term makes no measurable difference because the safety
filter already forbids any move that would create an articulation point, so
on an executed trajectory the quantity it measures is nearly constant and
the term has almost no variance to act on: the constraint is enforced by the
filter, and the reward term is a readout of it.  The degraded-service term
is close to inert for the same reason, since the filter keeps the graph
connected whenever the active set is unchanged.  In other words, the terms
that look as though they should matter most are the ones the hard
constraints have already discharged, and the terms that do matter are the
ones the filter cannot enforce: who covers what, and how the energy is
spent.

%% CREDIT

The per-agent arm reaches $0.613$ against $0.638$ for
the shared one, a difference of $-0.025$ in persistent coverage,
so the cheapest modification in the direction the diagnosis points is not
sufficient.  The scope of that statement is the one set out above: arm~B
supplies each agent with its own baseline and unshares the actor, and
neither of those changes the fact that the reward the gradient sees is the
same team reward for every agent.

That is a useful negative result rather than a frustrating one, for two
reasons.  It is consistent with the literature's own position: the
per-agent estimators that do change the signal are not trivial
modifications, and the closest coverage-specific evidence
\cite{sun2023} uses a per-agent difference utility rather than a per-agent
advantage estimator, so the question on a persistent-coverage task is
genuinely open.  And it narrows the search: a reader who wants to close
the gap now knows that the answer is not ``use a local critic''.

We therefore report the result as it stands: the credit assignment remains
the best-supported explanation for the gap between the learned controller
and the reactive heuristic, the two alternatives that the framework's other
controlled arms rule out are ruled out, and the cheapest available remedy
for the credit assignment does not close it.  The remedies that change the
signal --- a difference reward, a counterfactual baseline, a per-agent
value iteration --- are implementations of different algorithms, and we do
not claim to have implemented or evaluated them here.
