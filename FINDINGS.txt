SECTION A -- CHECKED AND FOUND CORRECT
================================================================================
  These were verified and need no change. Listed so the effort goes to the
  items that matter.

A1  The RL core is mathematically sound. Verified against Schulman et al.
    2017 (PPO, arXiv:1707.06347) and Schulman et al. 2016 (GAE,
    arXiv:1506.02438):

      - TD residual, Eq. (30) at L532-L533:
        delta_t = R_t + gamma V(s_{t+1}) - V(s_t)
        Matches PPO Eq. (12) exactly. Sign, gamma power, and bootstrap all
        correct.

      - GAE, Eq. (31) at L535-L537:
        A_hat_t = sum_{l=0}^{T-t-1} (gamma lambda)^l delta_{t+l}
        CORRECT, and the truncation index T-t-1 is more correct than the
        printed PPO paper, which has an off-by-one at Eq. (11). The absence
        of a 1/(1-gamma lambda) normalisation is also correct -- that factor
        cancels in the derivation (GAE paper Eqs. 16-18).

      - Clipped surrogate, Eq. (32) at L545-L550: correct against PPO Eq. (7).
        Clip range [1-eps, 1+eps] is standard.

      - Horizon consistency: T-1 in the objective (Eq. 2) with T-t-1 in GAE
        is the correct pairing for a T-transition rollout with one bootstrap
        state s_T.

A2  Coverage model, Eqs. (7)-(11) at L249-L266. All correct:
      x_id(t) = 1[||p_i - q_d|| <= R_cov]
      n_d(t)  = sum_{i in V_t^act} x_id(t)
      C_t     = (1/M) sum_d min(n_d/K, 1)
      U_t     = (1/M) sum_d 1[n_d < K]
    C_t = 1 if and only if n_d >= K for all d. Both metrics are sound.

A3  Connectivity model, Eqs. (12)-(13) at L313-L321. The articulation-point
    indicator and the Crit_t average are correct definitions. (The SYMBOL is
    reused -- see C4 -- but the mathematics is right.)

A4  Battery model, Eqs. (14)-(15) at L327-L339. The clip-to-[0, B_max]
    update and the action-dependent power split are correct. The return
    condition "E_i(t) <= E_i^req(t)" at L341 has the correct inequality
    direction.

A5  Charging assignment, Eq. (18) at L375-L383. A correct linear assignment
    problem: the constraints sum_s y_is <= 1 and sum_i y_is <= 1 enforce
    one-to-one matching, which is the right model for a pairwise assignment
    solvable by the Hungarian algorithm (Kuhn 1955; Munkres 1957, O(n^3)).

A6  Recovery time, Eq. (22) at L398-L401. tau_f = inf{tau >= 0 : n_d(t_f+tau)
    >= K for all d} is correct, and the report correctly handles the empty
    set (see A7).

A7  Four self-stated limitations that are mathematically correct and should
    be preserved verbatim:

      L116  "This is a survey-derived gap statement, not a claim that no
            related component has ever appeared in the broader literature."

      L394  "Connectivity is not assumed to be perfectly predictable from
            one-hop observations... This avoids claiming a global

            point from 1-hop information.

      L402  "If recovery is impossible under the available fleet, the event

            Correct handling of the infimum over an empty set.

      L457  "These are implementation parameters for the proposed simulator

A8  Action-space count is internally consistent. L66 states six actions and

C1  [HIGHEST] dt, d_safe and v_max are mutually inconsistent
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  LOCATION : Table IV, .tex L475 (dt = 1 s), L476 (v_max = 5 m/s),
             L477 (d_safe = 5 m); constraint at Eq. (20), .tex L389-L391

  THE CHECK
    Two UAVs converging head-on at 5 m/s each close at 10 m/s. Between two
    consecutive control steps they move 10 m relative to each other. The
    separation constraint is 5 m. A pair can pass from +7 m apart to -3 m
    apart with no sampled violation.

    Required stopping distance at a = 2 m/s^2:
      d_stop = v^2 / (2a) = 100 / 4 = 25 m
    This is 5x the declared d_safe of 5 m.

    Resolution of the separation constraint requires
      2 * R_cov must be resolvable in at least 2 d_safe steps, and
      the per-step relative displacement 2 * v_max * dt must be
      much smaller than d_safe.

      as printed:  2 * 5 * 1 = 10 m per step  vs  d_safe = 5 m

  WHY THIS MATTERS
    This is an internal logical contradiction, visible by comparing Table IV
    against Eq. (20). It does not require any external knowledge to find.
    A discrete-time simulator with this step size cannot enforce the
    constraint it claims to enforce.

  SOURCES
    Datsko, Nekovar, Penicka, Saska, "Energy-Aware Multi-UAV Coverage Mission
    Planning With Optimal Speed of Flight," IEEE RA-L 9(3):2893-2900, 2024,
    doi: 10.1109/LRA.2024.3358581 -- uses continuous TOPPRA time
    parameterisation with an analytic a_max-limited turn model, not
    stepwise motion.

    UAV AoI / MEC literature convention: time slots of 0.1-1 s, position
    update p(t+tau) = p(t) + v*tau (e.g. arXiv:2205.00692).

  FIX
    dt = 0.2 s  (1 m/step, 5x finer than d_safe = 5 m), or
    d_safe = 10-15 m together with dt = 0.2 s.
    If a 1 s decision interval is needed, decouple it: update positions at
    0.2 s, run the policy every 1 s, and state both in Table IV.

C4  [HIGH] R_cov = 15 m cannot satisfy the paper's own K-coverage condition
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  LOCATION : Table IV, .tex L472; condition stated at L268; Eq. (8) at L255

  THE CHECK
    K-coverage of all M sensors requires at least K*M = 40 sensor-UAV
    incidence pairs. With uniform sensors in area A, one UAV covers on
    average (M/A) * pi * R_cov^2 sensors.

      expected pairs = N * (M/A) * pi * R_cov^2
                     = 5 * (20/10,000) * pi * 15^2
                     = 5 * 0.002 * 706.858 = 7.07
      required pairs = K*M = 40
      ratio          = 7.07 / 40 = 0.1767

    In area units:
      supplied  N * pi * R_cov^2 = 3,534.29 m^2
      required  K * |Omega|      = 20,000.00 m^2

    K_max = floor(0.1767) = 0. The K = 1 condition also fails.

    Solving N pi R^2 = K A for the radius:
      R_cov = sqrt(K |Omega| / (pi N)) = sqrt(20,000 / 15.708) = 35.68 m
    With congruent-disk packing density delta = pi/(2 sqrt 3) = 0.90690:
      R_cov = sqrt(20,000 / 14.245) = 37.47 m

    Grid check, 5 x 4 sensors over 100 x 100 m, spacing 20.0 x 25.0 m: a
    15 m disk contains 1 sensor. N = 5 gives 5 pairs against 40 required.
    Best-case placement does not help.

  WHY THIS MATTERS
    With the ratio below 1, n_d(t) >= K is unsatisfiable for any policy. The
    dominant reward term lambda_O * U_t at Eq. (28) is then constant at its
    maximum, every baseline scores identically, and the coverage experiment
    produces no learning signal.

  FIX
      .tex L471  Omega   100 x 100 m  ->   80 x 80 m
      .tex L472  R_cov       15 m    ->    35 m
    With these: N pi R^2 = 19,242 m^2 against K A = 12,800 m^2, ratio 1.50.
    After one UAV fails the ratio is 1.20, still above 1, so the reserve UAV
    is a safety factor rather than the only route to feasibility.
    R_cov minimum for these values: 28.55 m counting, 29.98 m packing.

  SOURCES for the bound
    Classical disk-covering result: the densest packing of congruent disks
    has density delta = pi/(2 sqrt 3) = 0.90690, giving the covering count
    N >= 2A / (3 sqrt 3 r^2). Note the constant 1/(2 sqrt 3) is a PACKING
    density and is sometimes misread as a covering bound; the covering
    constant is 2/(3 sqrt 3). The necessary condition used above,
    N pi r^2 >= K A, is the weaker area-counting statement and is sufficient
    for the purpose of Table IV.

C8  [HIGH] Value normalisation is absent -- MAPPO's most important component
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  LOCATION : not present anywhere in the document

  MAPPO'S FINDING
    "we find that using value normalization never hurts training and often
    improves the final performance of MAPPO significantly. In Spread, where
    the episode returns range from below -200 to 0, value normalization is
    CRITICAL to strong performance."
    -- Yu et al. 2022, Sec. 5.1, Suggestion 1 of 5

    The mechanism: standardise value-function targets with a running mean
    and standard deviation; the network regresses to normalised targets; the
    running mean is used to DENORMALISE the network output when computing
    GAE.

  WHY THIS MATTERS HERE
    Eq. (28) at L425-L430 sums six weighted terms, so R_t is plausibly of
    order 10^2. Unnormalised value regression at that scale is the most
    likely reason a MAPPO reproduction underperforms.

  SOURCES
    C. Yu et al., NeurIPS 2022, Sec. 5.1, Algorithm 1 (PopArt steps).
    M. Hessel et al., "Ray RLlib: A composable library for distributed
    deep RL," ICLR 2019, arXiv:1905.12865 -- the PopArt reference.

  ALSO ABSENT (all standard in MAPPO, all additive)
    - Advantage normalisation              Sec. 3.3
    - Entropy bonus, sigma = 0.01           Appendix A
    - Orthogonal init, gain 0.01            Algorithm 1 line 1
    - Gradient clipping, max-norm 10        reference config.py
    - Observation LayerNorm                 reference config.py
    - <= 15 epochs, minibatch = 1           Sec. 5.3, Suggestion 3
    - epsilon < 0.2, same value for policy and critic   Sec. 5.4

C9  [MEDIUM] The value target is not stated to be detached
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  LOCATION : L556  "where R_hat_t = A_hat_t + V_phi(s_t)"

  THE MATHEMATICS IS CORRECT
    A_hat_t is the GAE advantage; A_hat_t + V(s_t) is exactly the
    TD(lambda) lambda-return:
      A_hat_t = G^lambda_t - V(s_t)   =>   R_hat_t = G^lambda_t
    This is the standard semi-gradient target and is unbiased when V is
    exact. Verified by expansion against Sutton and Barto, Reinforcement
    Learning: An Introduction, 2nd ed., Ch. 12.

  THE OMISSION
    The document does not state that R_hat is treated as a CONSTANT during
    the critic update. If gradients flow through it, the critic chases a
    self-referential target. The standard implementation computes the
    returns once into a buffer before the update loop.

    There is also a known discrepancy to be aware of: MAPPO's Appendix A
    prose calls R_hat "the discounted reward-to-go" (Monte Carlo), while the
    reference code computes gae + value_preds, i.e. the lambda-return. The
    report as written matches the CODE, not the prose. Say so explicitly,
    or a reader will "correct" it wrongly.

  SOURCES
    J. Schulman et al., "High-Dimensional Continuous Control Using
    Generalized Advantage Estimation," ICLR 2016, arXiv:1506.02438, Eq. 16.
    R. Sutton and A. Barto, Reinforcement Learning: An Introduction, 2nd ed.,
    MIT Press 2018, Ch. 12 (lambda-returns).
    MAPPO reference code, onpolicy/utils/shared_buffer.py line 258.

  FIX
    Add one sentence: "The target R_hat is computed once per rollout and
    treated as a constant; gradients are not propagated through it."

C10 [MEDIUM] The parameter-sharing justification names the wrong condition
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  LOCATION : L528
    "Parameter sharing is appropriate because the active UAVs have the same
     action semantics and physical model."

  THE ACTUAL CONDITION has two parts:
    (i) the environment is equivariant to permutations of agents --
        identical observation and action spaces AND identical
        transition/reward structure; and
    (ii) each agent's own observation identifies it, so a shared network can
         still specialise.
    Foerster et al. state this directly: "the agents can still behave
    differently because they receive different observations... In addition,
    each agent receives its own index i as input, allowing it to specialise."
    Condition (ii) is absent from the report's justification.

  THERE IS ALSO A FORMAL RESULT AGAINST IT
    Fu, Yu, Xu, Yang, Wu, "Revisiting Some Common Practices in Cooperative
    Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning," ICML 2022, arXiv:2206.07505,
    Theorem 4.2: a shared policy provably CANNOT learn an optimal policy for
    the XOR game. MAPPO cites this itself in its own Appendix C.2.
    Christianos et al., ICML 2021, reach a compatible conclusion: "naive
    parameter sharing... can be detrimental to final convergence in many
    environments."

  DOMAIN NOTE
    If all N UAVs are active and identical, sharing is well motivated. If
    any are grounded, docked, or hold heterogeneous roles, the agents are
    non-homogeneous; MAPPO explicitly does NOT share parameters in that case
    (MPE Comm) and the ICML 2022 result applies directly. Note that m_i
    (operating mode) at L180 may encode a role.

  FIX
    Restate using conditions (i) and (ii), cite Foerster 2016, and cite the
    ICML 2022 result as the known limitation. Cite MAPPO Appendix C.2 Table 4
    for the empirical gap, e.g. MMM2 90.6 with sharing versus 13.0 without.

C12 [MEDIUM] Pad occupancy is in the state but not in the observation
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  LOCATION : L177 (state) versus L185 (observation)

  Q_t in {0,1}^S appears in the global state at L177. It does NOT appear in
  o_i(t) at L185, nor in Table II at L202-L209.

  WHY THIS MATTERS
    Section III-H states the flow "RL request -> Station assignment ->
    Return trajectory" (L229-L235). The ACTOR issues the request. The critic
    sees pad occupancy; the actor does not. With S = 2 pads and N = 5 UAVs,
    several UAVs can be energy-critical simultaneously. An actor that cannot
    observe pad occupancy cannot distinguish "pads free, charge now" from
    "pads busy, stay and cover", so it cannot learn to defer. This is a
    missing input, not a training difficulty.

  FIX
    Append a per-pad vector to Eq. (4):
      q_i(t) = [ d_i1, ..., d_iS, u_i1, ..., u_iS, h_i ]
    where d_is is the distance to pad s, u_is its occupancy, and h_i flags
    whether the UAV already has a pending request. For S = 2 this adds 5
    scalars.

    This preserves decentralised execution: an occupied pad has a UAV docked
    at it, and that UAV is within R_comm, so occupancy is detectable by the
    same neighbour-exchange mechanism already used for rho_i at L188.

  RELATED, at L604
    "A recurrent policy should be introduced only if experiments show that
    instantaneous observations do not provide sufficient temporal
    information."
    The trigger cannot fire here: the information is definitionally absent
    from o_i, not empirically absent. Either fix the observation as above, or
    make the actor recurrent and state what the recurrent state encodes.

C14 [MEDIUM] Physical parameters in Table IV
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  LOCATION : Table IV, .tex L468-L482

  C14a  B_max = 30,000 J and P_hover = 50 W do not correspond to one aircraft
         Endurance = 30,000/50 = 600 s = 10 min hovering. Fitting
         P_hover ~ C m^1.3 to DJI data gives 51.6 W at 0.5 kg AUW, so 50 W is
         a valid light-platform figure, but that class packs 18-45 Wh
         (64,800-162,000 J), not 8.33 Wh.
         Reference points: DJI Mini 3, 18.1 Wh, 34 min; DJI Mavic 2, 43.6 Wh,
         24 min hover; DJI Phantom 4 Pro, 89.2 Wh, ~30 min.
         Datsko et al. (doi: 10.1109/LRA.2024.3358581) use a 130 Wh energy
         budget with P_hover = 426.03 W on a 4.5 kg Tarot T650, validated
         against 7 real flights.
         FIX: B_max = 60,000 J gives 20 min hover and is internally
         consistent with 50 W. Or keep 30 kJ and drop P_hover to ~15 W.

  C14b  P_move / P_hover = 75/50 = 1.5 is too high
         Datsko et al. measured P_r / P_h = 465.23 / 426.03 = 1.09, with
         P_r at the energy-optimal 8.39 m/s. Forward flight adds a v^3
         profile-drag term that is small for a multirotor at 5 m/s.
         FIX: P_move = 55-60 W, cite Datsko et al.

  C14c  Charge rate 0.01 per step is ~36 C; 0.001 per step is ~3.6 C

C16 [HIGH] Three further symbol collisions in the reward
